All three eventually, but she actually wanted to explain a change to our shared garden schedule.

> 最终这三个人都在一起了，但她实际上想解释一下我们共享花园时间表的变化。

  

A 2-minute call cleared it up.

> 2分钟的通话就解决了。

  

So you spent longer worrying about the message than you spent talking about the garden.

> 所以你花在担心消息上的时间比花在谈论花园上的时间还要长。

  

Did she realize those words had sounded serious to you?

> 她是否意识到这些话对你来说听起来很严肃？

  

She didn't.

> 她没有。

  

In her head, she was making a friendly suggestion.

> 她的脑海里，正在提出一个友好的建议。

  

I couldn't hear that part while I was staring at my phone.

> 当我盯着手机时，我听不到那部分。

  

That's today's topic, moving beyond text messages in English.

> 这就是今天的主题，超越了英语短信的范围。

  

When would a short call make something easier and how do you begin one?

> 什么时候简短的通话会让事情变得更容易？你如何开始通话？

  

Exactly.

> 确切地。

  

And we aren't going to tell everyone to call instead of text.

> 我们不会告诉每个人都打电话而不是发短信。

  

I like messages.

> 我喜欢留言。

  

They let me answer when I've got a moment.

> 他们让我在有空的时候回答。

  

Me too.

> 我也是。

  

If you ask me for an address, I'd rather have it written down.

> 如果你问我地址，我宁愿把它写下来。

> 否则，我会记住街道并完全发明门牌号。

  

Written details can be useful,

> 书面细节可能有用，

> 但有时，当真正的问题需要问题、答案时，我们会不断交换信息，

> 另一个简单的问题。

> 每条消息都比最后一条消息晚 10 分钟到达。

> 当你问起的时候

  

where to meet, you've both started cooking dinner somewhere else.

> 这就是我邻居发生的事情。

  

I asked, "Is everything okay?"

> 她答应了，但又问我什么时候回家。

  

That made me more confused.

> 这让我更加困惑。

  

Because she knew what she wanted to discuss and you still didn't.

> 因为她知道她想讨论什么，而你还不知道。

  

A little context would have helped before either of you picked up the phone.

> 在你们中的任何一个人拿起电话之前，了解一些背景知识会有所帮助。

  

Definitely.

> 确实。

  

Something like, "Can I call you about the garden schedule?

> 比如，“我可以打电话给你询问花园的时间表吗？

  

Nothing's wrong.

> 没什么问题。

  

It'll only take a couple of minutes."

> 只需要几分钟。”

> 我立刻就明白了。

  

That's a useful difference.

> 这是一个有用的区别。

  

"Can I call you?

" asks permission, but adding the subject helps the person know

> " 请求许可，但添加主题可以帮助该人知道

  

what kind of conversation they're agreeing to.

> 他们同意进行什么样的对话。

  

Right.

> 正确的。

  

They might need to find a document, step somewhere quieter,

> 他们可能需要找到一份文件，走到一个更安静的地方，

> 或者只是决定他们当时是否有注意力。

  

So, a call can be helpful without being a surprise.

> 因此，打电话可能会有所帮助，而不会让您感到意外。

  

I sometimes send, "Would now be a good time for a quick call about tomorrow?"

> 有时我会发送这样的信息：“现在方便就明天的事宜进行快速通话吗？”

  

That sounds natural.

> 这听起来很自然。

  

If tomorrow could mean several different things, I'd name the plan.

> 如果明天可能意味着几个不同的事情，我会命名该计划。

  

"About tomorrow's class."

> “关于明天的课。”

  

Or, "About picking up the table."

> 或者，“关于收拾桌子。”

  

True.

> 真的。

  

The person shouldn't have to solve a little mystery before answering.

> 在回答之前，这个人不应该必须解开一些谜团。

  

And if it isn't urgent, saying that can help them feel less rushed.

> 如果事情不紧急，这样说可以帮助他们感觉不那么匆忙。

  

Yes.

> 是的。

  

My neighbor and I eventually spoke after I got home.

> 我回家后最终和我的邻居进行了交谈。

  

She started laughing when I admitted I'd been reviewing every possible problem with my flower pots.

> 当我承认我一直在检查我的花盆可能存在的所有问题时，她开始大笑。

  

Were the flower pots innocent?

> 花盆是无辜的吗？

  

I feel we should let them know the investigation's finished.

> 我觉得我们应该让他们知道调查已经完成。

  

What was the actual change to the schedule?

> 日程安排的实际变化是什么？

  

She wanted to swap the days we watered the garden because she had an early appointment.

> 她想交换我们给花园浇水的日子，因为她有一个提前的约会。

  

We needed to check who'd be there over the weekend.

> 我们需要检查周末谁会在那里。

  

That sounds easier to sort out in one conversation than in six messages.

> 听起来，在一次对话中比在六条消息中更容易解决。

  

You could hear her question, explain your plans, and check what she'd meant.

> 你可以听到她的问题，解释你的计划，并检查她的意思。

  

Exactly.

> 确切地。

  

Then, I sent the agreed days in a short message afterward.

> 然后我就在短信里把约定好的日子发了过去。

  

We used the call to decide and the message to remember the details.

> 我们通过电话来决定，通过消息来记住细节。

  

I like that combination.

> 我喜欢这个组合。

  

You don't have to pick one kind of communication forever.

> 您不必永远选择一种沟通方式。

  

Different parts of the same task can need different things.

> 同一任务的不同部分可能需要不同的东西。

  

Especially when tone matters.

> 尤其是当语气很重要的时候。

  

If a written reply feels cold, you can ask what the person

> 如果书面回复感觉冷淡，你可以问对方是什么

> 意味着不要将您的第一次阅读视为唯一可能的阅读。

  

But, I wouldn't call immediately to demand an explanation of every short reply.

> 但是，我不会立即打电话要求对每一个简短的回复进行解释。

  

Sometimes, "Okay." just means the person understood and went back to making lunch.

> 有时，“好吧”。只是意味着该人理解并回去做午餐。

  

Absolutely.

> 绝对地。

  

Look at the situation.

> 看看情况吧。

> 如果你明白答案

> 没有什么问题需要解决，另一个电话可能只会打断你们两个下午的时间。

> 所以，我们要问的是

  

whether a conversation would help rather than whether somebody used enough friendly words.

> 谈话是否有帮助，而不是某人是否使用了足够友好的词语。

  

That's a more practical reason to reach for the phone.

> 这是拿起电话的更实际的原因。

  

Right.

> 正确的。

  

And for an English learner, knowing the reason can make a call feel smaller.

> 对于英语学习者来说，了解原因可以让通话变得更小。

  

You're asking about one thing.

> 你问的是一件事。

  

You aren't taking an English exam.

> 你不是在参加英语考试。

  

That's important.

> 这很重要。

> 你可以知道你想问什么，但仍然感到紧张

Yes, a message gives you editing time.

> 是的，一条消息会给您编辑时间。

  

A call asks you to listen and respond while the other person is there.

> 呼叫要求您在对方在场时倾听并做出回应。

So, you could glance at a note that says, "Table, Saturday at 10:00.

Can I come at 11:00?"

The conversation doesn't always follow the order on your paper.
